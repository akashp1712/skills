# Write Like an Amazonian — Reference

This skill implements **Rule 5** from Amazon's internal *Write Like an Amazonian* writing guide (circa 2018). The four answers sit inside a broader communication culture at Amazon.

![Write Like an Amazonian — four answers and writing tips](assets/write-like-an-amazonian.png)

## The four Amazon answers

If you get a question, reply with **one** of:

1. **Yes.**
2. **No.**
3. **A number.**
4. **I don't know** (and will follow up when I do).

This skill sharpens #4 into a committed deadline: **I don't know, but I'll know it by X.**

If you're uncomfortable saying option 4, you have work to do — define the owner, experiment, or artifact that will produce the answer.

## The full Write Like an Amazonian framework

Source: Amazon internal writing guidance, widely circulated as *Write Like an Amazonian* (published November 2018). Summarized in [Fact of the Day 1 — Write Like an Amazonian](https://www.factoftheday1.com/p/april-13-write-like-an-amazonian).

### 1. Use fewer than 30 words per sentence

Short sentences keep readers from getting lost. One idea per sentence.

**Word economy:**

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| with the possible exception of | except |
| due to the fact that | because |
| totally lacked the ability to | could not |
| until such time as | until |
| for the purpose of | for |

Use **subject–verb–object** sentences with clear doers and action. Avoid clutter phrases.

### 2. Replace adjectives with data

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| we made the performance much faster | we reduced server-side p90 latency from 10 ms to 1 ms |
| we made on-time delivery much higher | we increased on-time delivery rate by 15 bps, from 97.10% to 97.25% |

### 3. Eliminate weasel words

Weasel words sound precise but hide weak evidence.

| Weasel word | Replace with |
|-------------|--------------|
| nearly all customers | 87% of Prime members |
| significantly better | +25 basis points (bps) |
| significantly expanded grocery | grew grocery selection by 30k ASINs |
| arguably the best | *(specific metric or delete)* |

Also avoid: *would help the solution*, *might bring clarity*, *should result in benefits*.

### 4. Pass the "So what?" test

Before including a paragraph, ask:

- If I deleted this, would the reader miss it?
- How does this enrich the reader's understanding?
- What are the implications of this claim?

If it fails, cut it or add data.

### 5. Answer with one of the four Amazon answers

Start substantive responses with **Yes**, **No**, **a number**, or **I don't know (and follow up when you do)**.

**Example (from Fact of the Day 1):**

> **Q:** How many job postings are open for your team?
>
> **A:** Six postings are open today, and I expect three requisitions to be approved by Friday.

## Related Amazon culture

### Narrative memos (2004)

Bezos banned PowerPoint for S-Team meetings because slides let presenters gloss over ideas. Narrative memos force better thought.

- [Bezos's 2004 email](https://www.businessinsider.com/jeff-bezos-email-against-powerpoint-presentations-2015-7)
- [Working Backwards — Narratives & Decision Making](https://workingbackwards.com/concepts/narratives-decision-making/)

### Working Backwards (product process)

Amazon's Working Backwards process starts with five questions — including **"How do you know what customers want or need? Challenge yourself to use data to back your thinking."**

- [Working Backwards at Amazon — Danny Sheridan](https://medium.com/fact-of-the-day-1/working-backwards-at-amazon-a303c3680aa3)
- Source: *Working Backwards* (Bryar & Carr, 2019)

The four answers and data-driven writing are complementary: one forces **how you answer**; the others force **how you write and decide**.
